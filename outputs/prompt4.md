# Prompt 4

> Prompt 4. Based on the new policy signals from Prompts 1–3, what do they imply for the fed funds rate path over the next 6–12 months? What could markets learn from the press conference that they couldn't get from the statement alone?

---

Evidence is only the two documents. **Inferences are labelled as such.** No market data or later events are used. Item references (A#, B#, Q#, #) point to Prompts 1–3.

## 1. Implied fed funds path, next 6–12 months

**Starting point:** 3¾–4 percent (midpoint 3.875 percent).

| Horizon | Implied path | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest of 2026 | **One more ¼-point hike is the base case**, to 4–4¼ percent. | SEP: "The median participant judges that the appropriate federal funds rate to be 4.1 percent at the end of this year" (p. 3). *My arithmetic:* 4.1 is above the current 3.875 midpoint and closest to the 4–4¼ range (midpoint 4.125). Supported by "Inflation risks are to the upside" (p. 3) and the unmet standard: "Today the FOMC decided that this standard has not been satisfied." (p. 3). | Moderate: it is the Committee's median, not the Chair's view. |
| 2027 (rest of the 12-month window) | **Hold at the higher level; no cuts implied.** | SEP: "and to remain there next year." (p. 3). Total PCE inflation is projected to fall "to 2.3 percent next year" (p. 3), still above 2 percent. | Moderate. |
| Upside risk | **More than one further hike.** | The Chair's own signals are more hawkish than the SEP alone: policy was not restrictive before the hike (A1/#2), inflation is the "predominant focus" (A5/#5), and the Committee was "hard pressed" to call even the pre-hike stance restrictive (B7). *Inference:* the Chair gave no projection ("I have not offered a projection of my own." p. 3), so his own preferred path could be above the median. | Low–moderate (borderline): inferred from tone, not stated. |
| Downside risk | **A pause rather than a second hike.** | Only weak dovish hooks: "I don’t believe that we need to do harm to the labor markets to achieve our objective." (p. 14), plus the reporter-cited SEP horizon of 2029 for reaching 2 percent (pp. 12–13). An AI task force "should report by the end of the year" (p. 14) and could change the supply-side view. | Low. Nothing in either document points to **cuts** within 12 months. |

**What would trigger each move (the reaction function, from the opening statement):**
- **Another hike** if the standard stays unmet: "We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed." (p. 2). Also if the watch list deteriorates: credit and financial conditions, relative price changes broadening, inflation compensation rising, or expectations drifting (p. 3).
- **A pause** if *trends*, not single releases, show inflation moving to target clearly and fast enough: "Trends matter. Data points are noisy." (p. 8).
- **Timing is deliberately unclear:** "I’m not in the forward guidance business." (p. 4); "we’ll have more to say about it. But I’m ill-prepared to prejudge those future actions." (p. 13). *Inference:* the direction is clearer (up or flat) than the timing of the next move.

**Net:** a modest further tightening bias. The most likely path is roughly one more hike, then a plateau through next year, with the risks tilted toward more tightening rather than less.

## 2. What markets learned that the statement alone did not provide

| What the statement gives | What the press conference adds | What markets can do with it |
|---|---|---|
| A hike "to 3-3/4 to 4 percent" (S¶1), with no view on the policy stance | Policy was *not restrictive*: "So we removed a dose of accommodation." (p. 1); the Committee shared the view (p. 7) | Read the hike as the start of normalising policy, not the end of tightening. |
| "Today’s policy action will support a timelier return" (S¶3), with no condition | An explicit test: confidence that inflation is moving to target "clearly and at sufficient speed" (p. 2), judged unmet (p. 3) | A yardstick for the next meetings: no clear improvement in the trend means more tightening. |
| "dual mandate" (S¶1), with no weights | "So our predominant focus is on the price-stability side of our mandate." (p. 2); labor "in good shape" (p. 2) | Weight inflation data above labor data when pricing the next moves. |
| Nothing on indicators | A watch list: credit conditions, broadening, inflation compensation, expectations (p. 3) | Track breakevens and inflation expectations as tightening triggers. |
| No numbers, forecasts or rate path | Inflation figures (total PCE about 3.6, core PCE about 3.2 percent; p. 2); SEP medians and the 4.1 percent rate path; "Inflation risks are to the upside" (p. 3) | A numerical anchor: about one more hike, then a hold (*inference*, see Section 1). |
| Nothing on how decisions are made | "I’m not a data-point-dependent guy." (p. 6); the neutral rate has no "operational effect" (p. 8); "I’m not in the forward guidance business." (p. 4) | Expect less reaction to single releases, no guidance on where hikes stop, and more uncertainty around each meeting. |
| Nothing on the SEP's authorship | "I have not offered a projection of my own." (p. 3); "those aren’t my forecasts" (p. 13) | Discount the dots as a guide to the Chair's own path. |
| Nothing on markets | "Sometimes the market tries to prejudge our outcomes." / "But today was our decision." (p. 5) | The Fed won't simply confirm what markets have priced in, which raises the risk of surprises. |
| A 12–0 vote | July's hold was only a "good majority" view (p. 2), so some members wanted to act earlier | A hawkish wing exists, which lowers the chance of an early pause. |
| Nothing on politics | "Part of the independence of the Federal Reserve is we stay in our lane." (p. 9) | Lower the expected chance of politically driven cuts. |
| Nothing on AI | Task force "should report by the end of the year" (p. 14) | A possible change to how the Fed reads supply-side growth around year-end. |

**What markets still could not learn:** the timing of the next move, where hikes will stop (deliberately, given that the neutral rate is ruled out as a guide), and anything on the balance sheet beyond "ample reserves".

**Confidence note:** I'm least sure about turning the SEP median (4.1 percent) into one more hike. It is the Committee's median, which the Chair explicitly disowns, and his refusal of forward guidance means the timing could slip.
