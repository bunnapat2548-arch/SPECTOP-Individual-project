## Which dominates

- **Raw count: a tie, 8 vs 8.**
- **Firm cases only: clarification dominates, 7 vs 3.** Five of the eight signals are borderline, against one of the eight clarifications. On firm cases, then, the Q&A is mainly clarification.
- **What kind of signals they are:** the three firm signals (Q2, Q6, Q8) concern *how* the Fed decides: no forward guidance, trends over single data points, no role for the neutral rate. They don't concern *where* rates are going. Only Q15 hints at the next meetings, and the Chair qualifies it at once.

**Confidence note:** I'm least sure about the five borderline signals (Q7, Q9, Q13, Q15, Q16). Moving any one of them to clarification breaks the tie in favour of clarification, so the raw count is fragile, while the firm-case result is not.
